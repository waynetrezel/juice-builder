# Juicebuilder — scroll-storyboard draft 01

**Purpose:** a premium fresh-food commercial told by scroll in an industrial-style kitchen. Exposed brick, charcoal cabinetry, black steel framing and a substantial work island establish the set. Warm focused downlights from pendants and ceiling spots shape the ingredients; there is no direct sunlight. The scroll position scrubs one continuous rendered sequence forward and backward. The visitor does not choose ingredients, adjust a recipe, rotate a model, or operate playback controls.

**Product status:** temporary unbranded concept bottle. Ingredient captions below are provisional sensory copy, not nutritional or health claims.

## Motion and layout notes

- Keep the scene visually continuous while copy changes with each chapter. Brick, dark cabinetry and overhead pendants remain a recognizable background motif.
- Let the fruit arrive as distinct hero subjects, then feed each into the same growing spiral.
- The final ingredient triggers the whole vortex to move into the blender; the blend and pour complete the single story arc.
- Light the ingredients with directional overhead pools and gentle fill; keep the industrial background darker and softly out of focus so produce remains appetizing.
- Use regular webpage text above or beside the render so captions remain readable and accessible.
- Make the story reversible with scrolling. Reduced-motion users should get a readable chapter list and a strong final still.

## Animatic status and next step

The first Blender animatic is now built in `juicebuilder-story-animatic.blend`. It has a reversible 264-frame timeline at 24 fps, simple proxy geometry, 11 chapter markers, and one low-resolution render for each chapter. `renders/juicebuilder-animatic-contact-sheet.png` shows the sequence; `story-site/?animatic=1` swaps those WebP renders into the scroll stage. The forms are placeholders: the fruit is not photoreal, the vortex is only staged with small pieces, and the blender, juice and bottles are silhouettes. The animatic checks order and rough pacing, not final visual quality or fluid simulation.

The aloe realism test remains a separate asset pilot: one generated leaf is in the kitchen scene, and a smaller GLB copy has been prepared for the website. The current asset still shows texture and shape artifacts and does not meet the hyper-real target.
